Protein is the number that matters most
Set at 2.1 g per kg of bodyweight. In a deficit this is what keeps the scale loss coming from fat rather than muscle, and it's the hardest target to hit by accident.
Carbs sit low, but not at zero
Held at your ceiling and best spent around resistance days. On Total Synergistics, The Challenge, Incinerator and the eccentric days, put most of them before and after training. On mobility days you'll naturally run lower.
Fat fills the rest
Whatever's left after protein and carbs. It won't drop below roughly 0.8 g per kg, which is the floor worth keeping for hormone production.
Expect about 0.9 lb a week
That puts 186 near 176 somewhere around week 11. If you're dropping faster than 1.5 lb a week for two weeks running, the deficit is too steep — eat more, not less.

Hold these three while you run it

Protein around 170 g a day
Roughly 2 g per kg at 186 lb. In an AJCN trial, 2.4 g/kg beat 1.2 g/kg for holding lean mass and losing fat during a deficit. Anywhere in that upper range works.
Deficit under 500 calories
A 2025 network meta-analysis on exercise during calorie restriction recommends staying inside 500 kcal/day when the goal is preserving lean mass. Deeper is where the cortisol problem you asked about starts.
Heavy enough that 8–12 is hard
Load is what signals your body to keep the muscle. On resistance days the last two reps should be a genuine fight. Note the weight you used so next round has a target.
30 minutes is enough
A recent trial found high-volume resistance work in a deficit spared no more lean mass than lower volume, though it did build more strength. Longer sessions cost recovery you'd rather spend elsewhere.
